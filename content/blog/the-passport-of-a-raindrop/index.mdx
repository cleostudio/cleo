Follow a raindrop long enough and nationalism starts to look underinformed. Water does not renew a passport. It changes phase. It evaporates from a warm sea, rides a storm, stalls against a mountain range, freezes into a season of snow, melts into a creek, joins a river that ignores the straight lines on political maps, and eventually finds another ocean with a different name.

The hydrologic cycle is usually taught as a tidy loop. Lived at planetary scale, it is a visa-free travel network with occasional disasters.

## Departure

Most of the sky's moisture begins as ocean. Heat lifts water into air; wind issues the boarding passes. From orbit, you can see the waiting rooms: cloud streets, tropical towers, the glitter of sunglint where open water still remembers the sun.

A drop's identity is temporary. As vapor it is anonymous. As cloud it joins a crowd. As rain it becomes local again - someone's drought ending, someone's flood beginning.

## Mountains

Orographic lift is bureaucracy with better views. Moist air meets a range, rises, cools, and drops its luggage on the windward side. The leeward side may stay dry enough to become desert. Whole climates are decided by that encounter. The raindrop does not choose; the mountain edits its itinerary.

Snowpack is delayed travel - water stored as bright capital until spring releases it into agriculture, hydropower, and argument. A winter that looks scenic is often next summer's politics in storage.

## Borders

Rivers make convenient borders precisely because they are already lines on the land. They also make awkward borders because the water keeps moving and the people on both banks keep needing it. Upstream use is downstream fate. Aquifers ignore fences even more thoroughly.

A raindrop that falls in one country can become political in another before lunch. Treaties try to schedule what weather refuses to schedule.

## Cities

Urban life is a set of tools for catching, diverting, and regretting water. Gutters, reservoirs, levees, treatment plants - all of them are attempts to schedule a substance that prefers improvisation. When storms intensify, the schedule fails in public.

Seeing rain as a traveler helps. Infrastructure is hospitality design for a guest that sometimes arrives with too many friends. The guest does not RSVP; it arrives as radar color and then as a street you cannot cross.

## Stamp

You do not need a satellite to participate. Drink a glass. Watch a gutter. Notice that fog is a cloud that decided your street was high enough. The passport of a raindrop is stamped on your windows whenever weather remembers the ocean. The drop itself is busy naming something older than any capital: that Earth is one watershed wearing many temporary labels.
