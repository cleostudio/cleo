A knowledge portal can feel cheap the moment every page reads like the same
model filled in a template. Cleo draws a hard line: guides are curated;
conversation is generated.

That sentence is the product. Everything else — static photographs, a small
Topics list, an agent that deep-links real routes — supports the feeling that
you are reading something someone stood behind, then asking follow-ups in a
place designed for follow-ups.

## Line

Country orientation lives in a maintained prose corpus. Drafts are checked for
length, recycled phrasing, and claims that go stale quickly. No sentence may
appear in two countries. The site never calls a model to render an Explore
page.

Space primers follow the same editorial instinct — stable orientation and
features, not a regenerated summary on each visit.

The checks are boring on purpose. Length caps stop a guide from becoming a
brochure. Recycled-phrasing rules stop the atlas from rhyming with itself.
Volatile claims — election outcomes, momentary rankings, anything that ages in
weeks — stay out of the orientation so a bookmark remains trustworthy. Tests
hold the corpus to that bar so a future edit cannot quietly reintroduce a
duplicate sentence across Japan and Kenya.

Generation still has a home on this site. It lives at [Cleo](/cleo), behind a
server route that calls OpenAI, streams a reply, and forgets the transcript
when you reload. Drafting tools may help editors offline when a new country
orientation is written once into the corpus. What we refuse is the pattern
where opening `/explore/[slug]` secretly asks a model to invent the page.

Photographs follow a parallel discipline. Explore frames are curated and
imported as local JPEGs; Space frames follow a NASA pass into the same kind of
static tree. Looking at a guide does not re-encode an image on demand, and it
does not scrape a random host. Curation happens before publish.

## Why

Readers should trust that a guide they bookmark next month still says the same
thing. Editors should be able to review every claim. And the AI agent stays
useful precisely because the rest of the site is not improvising underneath it.

Stability also makes the UI quieter. A field guide can use warm paper, passport
labels, and contact prints without racing a live summary. [Gallery](/gallery)
can unify atlas and Space photos because both catalogs are files we already
chose. [Topics](/topics) can stay short until a new subject meets the same bar.

If every surface were generated, deep links would mean less. Pointing at
[Iceland](/explore/iceland) or [Mars](/space/mars) only helps when those pages
are durable destinations. Curation is what makes a route worth naming out loud.

## Agent

[Cleo](/cleo) may search the web, reason, and answer in the moment. That is the
right place for freshness. When the durable answer already exists as a field
guide, the agent links to it instead of replacing it.

Ask for a comparison between [Europa](/space/europa) and another moon. Ask what
to notice in a Kenya photograph. Attach a frame and ask a vision question.
Generate a sketch when a diagram helps. None of that requires Explore or Space
to dissolve into chat. The catalogs stay on their shelves; the agent walks the
aisles with you.

Curated, not generated, is not a slogan against AI. It is a boundary that keeps
Cleo feeling like one portal with two tempos — pages you can trust, and a
conversation you can clear.
