Space on Cleo is not a news ticker. It is a shelf of evergreen primers for
bodies you can keep returning to — from Mercury to nearby deep space. The
voice matches Explore on purpose: calm orientation, stable features, curated
photographs, and no obligation to refresh the prose every time a mission
makes headlines.

We want a Space page to feel like a field notebook you could pack beside a
country guide. Same English-only portal, same editorial bar, different shelf.

![](./image-1.jpg#1280x1280 "A curated NASA photograph from the Mars guide")

## Shelves

The catalog groups into three shelves:

- Solar System — planets and the Sun
- Moons — major natural satellites, plus the ISS as a human outpost
- Deep Space — nearby galaxies and nebulae

Every subject renders at `/space/[slug]` with orientation, notable features,
and a short fact plate. The goal is the same as Explore: leave you oriented,
not overwhelmed.

The shelves are a reading aid, not a bureaucracy. Planets sit where you expect
them. Moons gather the satellites worth a primer of their own. Deep space
holds the nearby galaxies and nebulae that still feel within reach of a short
guide. The ISS belongs with moons in the catalog sense — a human outpost you
can treat as a subject — even though it is not a natural satellite.

Topics lists Space beside Countries as one of the two doors that open the
portal today. From [Topics](/topics) or the homepage you can step into the
full shelf at [Space](/space), then drill into a single body when you are
ready to read.

## Photos

Space photographs come from curated NASA sources and ship as static files under
`/images/space/`. Like the country atlas, they are imported ahead of time and
served with browser `srcset` — no account, no CDN rewrite, no surprise
re-encoding when you open a page.

Curation happens before publish. We pick frames that actually show the
subject, import optimized JPEG renditions, and validate the tree so a missing
file cannot silently ship. Originals stay off the public path. [Gallery](/gallery)
pulls these same frames onto one wall with Explore place photos, so a Mars
print and a Kenya landscape can share a browsing mood without sharing a guide
template.

Start with [Mars](/space/mars), [Europa](/space/europa), or the
[ISS](/space/iss). The full shelf lives at [Space](/space).

## Asking

Curious how two bodies compare? Ask [Cleo](/cleo). Matching turns can deep-link
the guides and, when the subject matches, show a curated photo inline.

The agent is the right place for “how does Europa’s ice differ from what we
see on Mars?” or “what am I looking at in this ISS frame?” Field guides stay
durable; conversation can chase the tangent. When a subject has a page here,
Cleo should name the real path instead of inventing one — the same contract
Explore enjoys for countries.

If you are new to the shelf, pick one Solar System body, one moon, and one
deep-space subject. Read them slowly. Then open Gallery and filter to Space,
or ask the agent to walk a comparison. The sky on Cleo is meant to stay
small enough to finish — and rich enough to revisit.
