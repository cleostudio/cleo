Among the moons worth a field guide of their own, [Europa](/space/europa) and
[Enceladus](/space/enceladus) keep showing up in the same sentence: ice above,
liquid water below, and a chance that chemistry has somewhere to run. They are
not twins. One is a Galilean world under Jupiter’s radiation; the other is a
small Saturnian moon that vents its ocean into space.

![](./image-2.jpg#1280x853 "Enceladus, bright and plume-active")

## Europa

Europa is large for a moon — about 1,560 km in radius — and locked to Jupiter in
a 3.55-day orbit. Its bright crust is young by planetary standards: ridges,
chaos terrain, and reddish stains mark places where the ice has broken and
refrozen. Beneath that shell, magnetic induction and geology point to a global
saltwater ocean kept liquid by tidal heat.

The interesting story is structural. Radiation from Jupiter makes the surface
hostile; if there is chemistry worth chasing, it would be below the ice. A
field-guide reading stays with that architecture — crust, ocean, tidal engine —
rather than any single mission headline.

## Enceladus

Enceladus is much smaller, about 252 km in radius, orbiting Saturn in roughly
1.37 days. Most of the moon looks cratered and cold. The south pole does not.
Tiger-stripe fractures mark a warm, active region; jets of water vapor and ice
grains erupt from those troughs and feed Saturn’s diffuse E ring.

Those plumes matter because they sample the ocean. Cassini flew through them
and found salts and organics — evidence that liquid water contacts rock below
the shell. Orientation here is contrast: a world you can hold in a mental model,
yet one that punches above its size for ocean science.

## Compare

| | Europa | Enceladus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| System | Jupiter | Saturn |
| Radius | ~1,561 km | ~252 km |
| Ocean clue | Magnetic induction, geology | Plumes that vent the ocean |
| Surface | Ridged, chaos terrain | Tiger stripes at the south pole |
| Hazard | Intense Jovian radiation | Cold, tiny, plume-active |

Both are ocean moons. Europa is the larger, quieter vault — water inferred under
a thick lid. Enceladus is the sampler — water escaping where the crust cracks.
That difference shapes how you read a photograph: Europa’s stains and chaos are
the ice talking; Enceladus’s plumes are the ocean talking.
