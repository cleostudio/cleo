Some days you want prose. Some days you want to wander through photographs.
[Gallery](/gallery) is for the second mood. It is the visual twin of the field
guides: the same curated frames, arranged so images lead and reading follows
when you are ready.

We did not want two photo products — one for countries, one for the sky —
fighting for attention in the chrome. Places and bodies already share a job on
this portal: show you something real, then invite you back to the guide that
explains it.

## One wall

Gallery unifies two catalogs:

- Place photos from country Explore guides
- Body photos from Space guides

Filters let you narrow by origin without splitting the site into two photo
apps. `/photos` permanently redirects here so old links still land on the same
wall.

Both catalogs ship as static local JPEGs with browser `srcset`. Explore frames
come from a Wikimedia Commons curation pass into `/images/atlas/`. Space frames
come from a NASA curation pass into `/images/space/`. Gallery does not fetch a
third-party host at runtime; it reads the same files the guides already
publish. That is why the wall can feel continuous even though the subjects sit
on different shelves.

A filter is a lens, not a fork in the product. You can skim only places, only
space, or leave the wall open and let a fjord sit beside a moon. The point is
one composition you can return to — not a media library account, not a
slideshow that forgets where it came from.

## Why separate

Guides keep a reading rhythm — orientation first, then prints. Gallery flips
that: images lead, and each frame can send you back to its subject. The two
surfaces share assets; they do not share jobs.

On an Explore page you meet a country through warm paper labels and a short
orientation before the contact prints. On a Space page you meet a body the
same way. Gallery skips the passport voice on purpose. Here the photograph is
the headline. Caption and subject link do the quiet work of naming what you
are seeing and where to read more.

Keeping those jobs apart protects both moods. If every guide opened as a
masonry wall, orientation would get lost. If Gallery forced a full essay above
the fold, wandering would feel like homework. We would rather let you choose
the door: Topics for the map, Explore or Space for a primer, Gallery when you
want to look first.

Writing on this site stays in [Writing](/blog) — essays about how the portal
thinks — so Gallery never has to double as a blog index. The agent at
[Cleo](/cleo) can talk about a frame you just saw; the wall itself stays
visual.

## Next

If a photograph makes you want the story, open its Explore or Space guide. If
you want a walking tour in conversation, ask [Cleo](/cleo) about the place or
body you just saw.

A simple path: wander until something holds your eye, follow it into
[Japan](/explore/japan) or [Mars](/space/mars) or whichever subject the frame
belongs to, then bring a comparison back to the agent. Gallery is not a dead
end. It is the middle of a loop between looking, reading, and asking — one
wall for places and sky.
