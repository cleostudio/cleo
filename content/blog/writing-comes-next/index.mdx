Field guides answer what a place or body is. Writing answers how this portal
thinks — and, over time, subjects that deserve an essay instead of a passport
page.

![](./image-1.jpg#1280x853 "Morocco, from the Explore atlas")

## Role

[Writing](/blog) is MDX in the repository: titles, dates, and prose you can read
without a database. It is the same publishing path a future encyclopedia-like
layer can grow into — still English-only, still reviewed before it ships.

These early posts stay close to the product. They explain Explore, Space,
Gallery, Topics, and the agent so the site has a voice besides UI chrome. The
tone is calm and collective: what you can open, what stays stable — not a
founder diary.

Writing does not generate country orientation at request time. That corpus stays
curated elsewhere. Writing also does not replace [Cleo](/cleo). Conversation,
web search, and images in the moment belong to the agent. Essays are for
explanation you can still cite next month.

## Shape

Expect short titles, short section headers, and links back into real routes. You
will not find personal year-in-reviews here. You will find the editorial
decisions that keep Cleo feeling like one composition.

A post should usually do one job: welcome you, draw the line between curated
guides and generated chat, explain why photographs stay local, or name what
Topics holds today. Covers and in-post images are local files beside the MDX.
If a sentence does not help you understand the portal, it belongs somewhere
else.

## Ahead

As Topics expands, Writing can hold comparisons, methods, and longer primers
that do not fit a field-guide template. Until then, start with
[Welcome to Cleo](/blog/welcome-to-cleo) and wander outward.

The long game is an encyclopedia-like layer that still feels like Cleo: curated
where it should be curated, conversational where conversation helps, and honest
about what exists today. Countries and Space are the first shelves. Writing is
the hallway that explains the architecture before more doors open.

When you finish an essay, the next click should be concrete — a country, a
body, a wall of photographs, or a question for the agent. The words are here to
send you back into the portal.
