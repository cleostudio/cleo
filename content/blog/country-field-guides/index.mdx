Each Explore guide is a field notebook for one country: enough orientation to
stand on solid ground, then curated photographs of real places. The pages are
English-only, evergreen, and meant to be bookmarked — not regenerated when you
refresh.

We reach for a passport metaphor on purpose. Labels, hairline rules, and a
warm paper layout signal that you opened a guide, not a news CMS. The job is
orientation first; headlines can wait elsewhere.

![](./image-1.jpg#1280x853 "A curated place photograph from the Japan guide")

## Format

Open any country at `/explore/[slug]`. The layout borrows passport labels and
hairline rules on purpose. You should feel like you opened a warm paper guide,
not a CMS article.

What you get on a typical page:

- A short orientation that sets place, climate, and character
- Stable facts that do not chase daily headlines
- A contact-print style photo sequence you can zoom

Orientation prose is curated in a maintained corpus, written once and checked
for length, recycled phrasing, and claims that go stale quickly. No sentence
may appear in two countries. The site never calls a model to render an Explore
page. That is the line between guides and conversation: the guide stays put;
[Cleo](/cleo) handles the live questions.

Facts stay conservative on purpose. Population figures and political weather
change; the field guide prefers character you can still trust next season. If
you need a fresher answer, the agent can search. If you need the durable
primer, the page you bookmarked is still the page.

## Atlas

Country records live in the site atlas. Photos are chosen for relevance first —
landmarks and landscapes that actually belong to the place — then imported as
local JPEG renditions. The page never fetches a third-party image host at
runtime.

The pipeline is deliberate: assemble the manifest, curate Wikimedia Commons
candidates with relevance first, import optimized JPEGs under
`/images/atlas/{slug}/`, then validate before deploy. Originals remain off the
public tree. The browser picks a width from `srcset`. [Gallery](/gallery)
reuses those same place frames beside Space photographs, so looking and
reading stay on one site.

Try a few starting points: [Japan](/explore/japan), [Iceland](/explore/iceland),
or [Kenya](/explore/kenya). Or search from the [homepage](/).

From [Topics](/topics) the Countries door opens the same atlas. Former
`/projects` URLs redirect there; Explore remains the reading surface for each
slug.

## Companion

When a guide answers the first questions and you still want more, ask
[Cleo](/cleo). The agent knows the Explore catalog and can deep-link the right
guide instead of inventing a path.

A natural loop is read, look, ask: finish the orientation, zoom a contact
print, wander Gallery if you want neighbors, then bring a comparison to the
agent — Japan beside Iceland, or a Kenya landscape beside a question the fact
plate does not cover. Matching turns may embed a curated atlas JPEG inline as
Markdown. The bytes were already on the site; the agent is only pointing at
them.

Explore is the country half of Cleo’s first chapter. Space is the other shelf.
Together they are enough to start — and steady enough to grow from.
