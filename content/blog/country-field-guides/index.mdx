An Explore page is a field notebook for one country: enough orientation to stand
somewhere, then photographs of places that actually belong there.

![](./image-1.jpg#1280x853 "Milford Sound, New Zealand")

## Format

Open any country at `/explore/[slug]`. Warm paper, passport labels, and hairline
rules are intentional. You should feel a guide in hand, not a CMS article.

A typical page gives you:

- A short orientation — place, climate, character
- Facts chosen to age slowly
- A contact-print sequence you can zoom

Orientation is curated once and held to a bar: no recycled sentences across
countries, no claims that spoil in weeks. Freshness belongs to conversation;
the guide stays put. Why that line exists is the subject of
[Curated, not generated](/blog/curated-not-generated).

## Places

Photographs are chosen for relevance first — landmarks and landscapes that
belong to the country — then published with the guide. [Gallery](/gallery)
reuses the same frames beside Space photographs when you want to look without
opening a primer.

Start with [Japan](/explore/japan), [Iceland](/explore/iceland), or
[Kenya](/explore/kenya) — or search from the [homepage](/). From
[Topics](/topics), the Countries door opens the same shelf. Space is the other
half of the first chapter, at [Space](/space).
