An Explore page is a field notebook for one country: enough orientation to stand
somewhere, then photographs of places that actually belong there.

![](./image-1.jpg#1280x853 "New Zealand, from the Explore atlas")

## Format

Open any country at `/explore/[slug]`. Warm paper, passport labels, and hairline
rules are intentional. You should feel a guide in hand, not a CMS article.

A typical page gives you:

- A short orientation — place, climate, character
- Facts chosen to age slowly
- A contact-print sequence you can zoom

Orientation is curated in a maintained corpus. Drafts are checked so no sentence
appears in two countries, and claims that spoil in weeks stay out. The site
never asks a model to render Explore. Freshness belongs to [Cleo](/cleo); the
guide stays put.

## Atlas

Country records live in the site atlas. Photographs are chosen for relevance
first, then imported as local JPEGs under `/images/atlas/`. Pages do not fetch a
third-party image host at runtime. [Gallery](/gallery) reuses the same frames
beside Space photographs, so looking and reading stay one portal.

Start with [Japan](/explore/japan), [Iceland](/explore/iceland), or
[Kenya](/explore/kenya) — or search from the [homepage](/). From
[Topics](/topics), the Countries door opens the same shelf.

## Companion

When the primer answers the first questions and you still want more, ask
[Cleo](/cleo). The agent knows the Explore catalog and should deep-link a real
path instead of inventing one. Matching turns can show a curated atlas
photograph inline.

Read, look, ask: finish the orientation, zoom a print, then bring a comparison
to the agent. Explore is the country half of Cleo’s first chapter. Space is the
other shelf.
