Cleo is a place to learn about the world and the sky without turning either into
a feed. Search a country, open a planet, wander photographs, then ask a
follow-up — all on one small English-only site.

![](./image-1.jpg#1280x1280 "Earth from the Space catalog")

## Portal

The homepage is a doorway. From there the map stays short on purpose:

- [Explore](/explore) for country field guides
- [Space](/space) for Solar System, moons, and deep space
- [Gallery](/gallery) for photographs from both shelves
- [Topics](/topics) for the subject list as it grows
- [Writing](/blog) for essays about how the portal thinks
- [Cleo](/cleo) for conversation on the same catalogs

Each route keeps one job. Explore orients. Space orients. Gallery lets images
lead. Topics names the doors. Writing explains the architecture. The agent
handles the questions a static page should not chase.

Older bookmarks still behave: `/photos` lands on Gallery, `/projects` on Topics.

## Voice

Field guides are curated. Country orientation is written once, checked for
length and recycled phrasing, and never invented when a page loads. Space
primers follow the same calm bar. Photographs ship as local files you can zoom
without an image account.

The agent is the flexible layer — web search, vision, image generation, and
links into real guides — while Explore and Space stay bookmarkable. Chat lives
in your browser and clears on reload. There is no login and no analytics trail.

## Start

Open [Explore](/explore) for a place, [Space](/space) for a body, or
[Cleo](/cleo) when you want to talk it through.

A good first hour: search a country, read the orientation, zoom a contact
print, drift through [Gallery](/gallery) until a Space frame catches you, then
ask the agent to compare what you just saw. The portal is quiet on purpose.
The next question is the point.
