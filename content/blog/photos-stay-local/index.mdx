Photographs are half of a field guide. They are also where modern sites often
grow fragile. Cleo keeps them boring on purpose: the bytes live on the site,
and runtime stays out of the image business.

![](./image-1.jpg#1280x851 "Peru, from the Explore atlas")

![](./image-2.jpg#1280x720 "Saturn, from the Space catalog")

## Pipeline

Country photographs are curated for relevance, then imported as optimized local
JPEGs. Space photographs follow a NASA curation pass into the same kind of
static tree.

Public files live under:

- `/images/atlas/{slug}/` for Explore places
- `/images/space/{slug}/` for Space bodies

Originals stay off the public tree. The browser chooses a width. There is no
account-gated image host and no request-time re-encode.

Relevance beats decoration. A landmark should belong to the country on the
page; a Space frame should show the body you opened. [Gallery](/gallery) and
the guides speak the same paths, so a frame is never “in the CMS” in one place
and “on a CDN” in another.

## Payoff

Pages keep working in previews without extra services. Guides stay reviewable
in git. The Gallery can unify both catalogs because both already speak one file
language.

Boring infrastructure is a product choice. It skips broken tokens, surprise
re-encodes, and layouts that wait on a remote host. Diffs show when a
photograph changes. [Cleo](/cleo) can embed a matching curated path as Markdown
because those paths are already site URLs — not scraped hotlinks.

OpenAI remains the only third-party API this portal depends on, and it belongs
to conversation, not to every image.

## Reading

Zoom a contact print on [Explore](/explore), wander [Gallery](/gallery), or ask
[Cleo](/cleo) about a subject that has a curated frame. The bytes were already
here.

Try the loop: open [Japan](/explore/japan) or [Mars](/space/mars), zoom until
the print feels close, filter Gallery by origin, then ask how the frame compares
to [Europa](/space/europa). The photograph does not need another vendor to
finish that sentence.
