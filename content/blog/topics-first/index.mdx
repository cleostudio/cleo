Cleo is meant to grow as a general-knowledge portal. Growth starts with a short
list, not an empty promise of everything.

We would rather name two doors you can open today than wallpaper the chrome
with subjects that do not yet meet the editorial bar. Topics is that short
list: a map of the portal, honest about what exists, and ready to lengthen when
a new shelf can stand beside the first ones.

## Now

[Topics](/topics) currently highlights two doors:

- **Countries** — the Explore atlas of field guides and place photographs
- **Space** — Solar System, moons, and deep-space primers

Those topics also appear on the homepage as discovery paths beside country
search and highlighted places.

Countries lead into [Explore](/explore): passport-style primers with curated
orientation, stable facts, and local JPEG contact prints. Space leads into
[Space](/space): the same calm field-guide voice for planets, major moons, the
ISS, galaxies, and nebulae. [Gallery](/gallery) sits beside both as one
filterable wall — places and sky sharing assets without sharing a reading
template.

The agent at [Cleo](/cleo) knows these catalogs too. When a question matches a
guide, the reply should deep-link a real path — `[Japan](/explore/japan)`,
`[Europa](/space/europa)` — instead of guessing. Topics is the index that makes
that promise legible: if it is on the list, the routes behind it are real.

## Later

More topics will land when they can meet the same bar: curated orientation,
honest media, and routes the agent can deep-link without guessing. Until then,
the catalog stays intentionally short.

Former `/projects` URLs redirect to Topics so old bookmarks still find a
subject index.

“More later” is not a tease. It is a constraint. A new topic should bring
English-only guides you can review, photographs that ship as static files, and
a home in Writing when the subject needs an essay instead of a passport page.
It should not require auth, a database, or a second analytics stack to exist.
OpenAI remains the only third-party API, and it belongs to the agent — not to
rendering a topic page.

Writing at [Writing](/blog) already explains the portal’s decisions while that
future grows. Essays can hold comparisons and methods that do not fit a
field-guide template. Topics names the doors; Writing narrates the
architecture; Explore and Space do the reading.

## Browse

Open [Topics](/topics) when you want the map of the portal. Open a topic when
you want the shelves inside it.

From Countries, search or open a guide — [Iceland](/explore/iceland),
[Kenya](/explore/kenya), or whichever place you came to learn. From Space,
start with [Mars](/space/mars) or the [ISS](/space/iss), then wander outward.
When photographs want to lead, switch to Gallery. When a question wants a
voice, ask Cleo.

Topics first means the portal stays navigable while it grows. Two doors are
enough to begin. The next door will open when it deserves a key.
