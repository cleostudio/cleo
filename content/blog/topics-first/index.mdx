Most knowledge sites grow by promising everything. Cleo grows by naming what you
can open today.

![](./image-1.jpg#1280x851 "The pyramids at Giza, Egypt")

## Two doors

[Topics](/topics) currently lists:

- **Countries** — into [Explore](/explore) field guides and place photographs
- **Space** — into [Space](/space) primers for planets, moons, and deep space

Those doors also appear on the homepage beside country search and highlighted
places. [Gallery](/gallery) sits beside both when you want photographs first.
The agent at [Cleo](/cleo) reads the same map: if a subject is on the list, a
real route should exist behind it.

A short catalog is easier to trust than a wallpaper of empty shelves. Two doors
are enough to begin a useful hour — [Egypt](/explore/egypt) or
[Mars](/space/mars) — without pretending the atlas already covers every subject
a person might ask.

## The bar

More topics arrive when they can meet the same standard as the first two:
curated orientation, honest photographs, and paths the agent can deep-link
without guessing. Until then the list stays short on purpose.

“More later” is a constraint, not a tease. A new topic should feel like another
shelf in the same library — curated guides, honest photographs, and paths the
agent can name — not a second product with half-finished stubs.

## How to browse

Open [Topics](/topics) when you want the map. Open a topic when you want the
shelves inside it.

From Countries, try [Iceland](/explore/iceland) or [Kenya](/explore/kenya). From
Space, start with [Saturn](/space/saturn) or the [ISS](/space/iss). When looking
should lead, switch to Gallery. When a question wants a voice, ask Cleo.

The next door opens when it deserves a key — and when Writing, Explore, and the
agent can all point at it without inventing a path.
